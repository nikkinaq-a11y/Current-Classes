SECTION 2: REVISIONS
===============================================================================

This version (story-v3) revises the previous version (story-v2). Both earlier
versions are preserved: version one and version two are included in this folder
as previous-version-1.html and previous-version-2.html, and are linked at the
bottom of the page so the progression can be traced.

--- ACCESSIBILITY: COLOR CONTRAST ---

I audited every text color in the page against the background (#0d0f12) using
the WCAG contrast formula. Three colors failed the AA standard of 4.5:1 for
normal text, so I corrected them:

   ELEMENT                 BEFORE            AFTER
   captions, small text    #6f7880 4.27:1    #8a939b 6.15:1
   red story beats, links  #c1554a 4.26:1    #cf6355 5.09:1
   table and box borders   #242a31 1.33:1    #5a6672 3.27:1

The red is the color carrying the emotional weight of the story, so it mattered
that it be legible rather than merely atmospheric. The new red is slightly
brighter but reads as the same color, and it now passes AA at normal text size.
Border contrast matters because the table borders are what separate one row of
data from the next.

--- ACCESSIBILITY: NOT RELYING ON COLOR ALONE ---

Links were previously distinguished from body text only by being red. Anyone
with a red-green color vision deficiency would not reliably see them as links.
All links are now underlined as well as colored.

--- ACCESSIBILITY: HEADING STRUCTURE ---

The three dramatic lines ("Her friend did not show up.", "Instead, she knew
absolutely nothing.", "No answer.") were marked up as <h3> headings purely
because I wanted them to look large and red. They are not section headings,
and a screen reader user navigating the page by heading would have heard them
announced as structural landmarks, which is misleading. They are now ordinary
paragraphs with a .beat class, so they look exactly the same but no longer
pollute the heading outline. The page now has one <h1> and six <h2> section
headings, in order, with nothing skipped.

--- ACCESSIBILITY: KEYBOARD ACCESS ---

1. Added a "Skip to the story" link as the first focusable element. It is
   hidden until focused, and lets a keyboard user jump past the audio player
   and the section-jump links straight into the narrative.
2. Added a visible yellow focus outline to every link, the audio player, and
   the iframe. Previously a keyboard user had no reliable indication of where
   they were on the page.
3. The faded "effectively disappear" line previously revealed itself only on
   mouse hover, which made it mouse-only content. It now also reveals on
   keyboard focus, and carries a title attribute.

--- ACCESSIBILITY: THE FADED LINE ---

The line "effectively disappear" is deliberately set at 1.95:1 contrast so that
it nearly vanishes into the background. This intentionally fails WCAG. I kept
the effect because the disappearance IS the meaning of that sentence, but I
mitigated it three ways: the text is fully present in the markup, so a screen
reader reads it normally; it reveals on both hover and keyboard focus; and I
added a visible caption underneath telling the reader the line is faded on
purpose and how to read it. A reader can no longer miss it without being told
it is there.

--- ACCESSIBILITY: TABLE AND MEDIA SEMANTICS ---

- Added scope="col" to both table headers so screen readers associate each
  data cell with the correct column.
- The audio caption now states that the music is optional and that the story
  reads the same with sound off, so the page does not depend on audio.
- The audio caption already made clear the track is atmospheric music and not
  a recording of my grandmother, which avoids a misleading impression.
- Both images carry descriptive alt text, plus width and height.
- The iframe has a title attribute describing its purpose.

--- READABILITY ---

Raised the base font size from 19px to 20px and set line-height to 1.7. The
previous version had no line-height at all, so paragraphs rendered tightly.

--- CLARITY OF INTERACTION ---

The iframe is the one part of the page a visitor has to figure out on their
own. I strengthened its instruction from "Click a thought." to explicitly
saying that clicking opens the thought in the box underneath, and that the
thoughts get worse in order from left to right. I also labeled the section
links with "Jump to a section:" so their purpose is stated rather than implied.

--- WHAT I WOULD DO NEXT WITH MORE TIME ---

- Make the layout respond properly to narrow phone screens. It currently uses
  a fixed maximum width, which is acceptable but not genuinely responsive.
- Offer a light-background version, since a dark page is harder to read for
  some people with astigmatism.
- Give the four "thoughts" a visual indication of which one is currently open,
  so a visitor can tell where they are in the sequence.
- Replace the fixed pixel image widths with relative sizing so the photos
  scale on small screens.
