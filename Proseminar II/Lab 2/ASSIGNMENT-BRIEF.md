# Lab 2 — "The Trouble with Tribbles": Assignment Brief

The instructor's assignment description, as posted to Canvas. Preserved here because it carries the
conceptual framing, not just the technical steps.

---

## The premise

> ["The Trouble with Tribbles" Star Trek (1967)](https://canvas.duke.edu/courses/83685/files/5397010/preview)
>
> In the image above, Captain Kirk (from the original "Star Trek" series) is enveloped by "Tribbles." In the
> episode that inspired the title of this assignment, these furry little creatures are brought aboard a
> spaceship and quickly multiply. Tribbles are not dangerous, but they are incredibly prolific. The show
> aired in 1967 and had a limited budget, so they barely move and make very little noise. The creators of
> these furry props did their best to at least make them seem like they could be a real species by changing
> the color and texture of their fur and making some of them larger than others.

Your task is to create many multiples of your own version of a tribble.

## The three parts

**1. Follow the Tribbles [Video Part 1](https://youtu.be/JQGPQEOYZcI?si=NQ6TmptDaI7z2-E9) and complete it.**
Shows how to add procedural geometry and colors onto a single object. The result can be a stand-alone part
of this lab, or you can integrate the "horns" into the network used for the final render.

**2. Watch Tribbles [Video Part 2](https://youtu.be/AbWWhpqjGSg).** Shows how to take two photogrammetry
models, cut them, and merge them with VDBs. The objects you merge act as "body options" that you can switch
out in the `Simplified Tribbles Project.hipnc` file. Also covers camera setup, rendering images, and
changing the HDRI environment that lights the scene.

**3. Open your copy of `Simplified Tribbles Project.hipnc`:**

- Explore the network by turning on render flags and watching what happens when you change parameters for
  different nodes. Become as familiar with how it works as you can by following the sticky-notes in the
  network.
- Paste or parametrically design new bodies into the scene and wire them into the three spots made
  available for you.
- Take the more complex horn designs from Part 1 and try to implement them in the network.
- Find where you can change the location and density of your tribbles and make them jumble about where you
  want them to go.
- **Render 3–4 views of your tribbles scene and turn them in.**
- *(Optional)* Create a second photogrammetry model to build a new hybrid.

## The conceptual framing

> The nature of that hybridity will be up to you to define. A biological metaphor like "hybridity" is only
> one form of combination, which means there are many ways to introduce combination, recombination, merging,
> blending, feature extraction, and deletion in order to create your new population of tribbles.

This is the part that isn't a technical instruction. The VDB union taught in Video 2 is one answer to
"how do two things become one thing," but it is not the only one, and the brief is explicitly inviting
others — subtraction, borrowing a single feature, repetition with variation.

## Deliverable

**3–4 rendered images** of a scene with a population of tribbles. Lighting and environment controlled by an
HDRI downloaded from [polyhaven.com/hdris](https://polyhaven.com/hdris).

The Part 2 video adds: four is the maximum, and the instruction is to commit to four considered
compositions rather than producing thirty variations.
